# Arabic glossary — BMGT Enterprise

Every recurring term has **one** Arabic rendering. If you add copy, take the term
from here rather than re-deciding it; two renderings of "valve" across two pages is
the fastest way to make a translated site look machine-made.

Register: Modern Standard Arabic, region-neutral, professional. The English voice is
restrained and concrete — short sentences, no marketing inflation. Keep that.

## Never translated (confirmed carve-out)

Company name, manufacturer marks, email addresses, phone numbers, URLs.

`BMGT Enterprise` · `Legrand` · `Black+Decker` · `FAG` · `Schneider Electric` ·
`Makita` · `Polycab` · `SKF` · `Janatics` · `ELGi` · `Kirloskar` · `Siemens` ·
`Sales@bmgt.ae` · `+971 54 777 8655` · `Cubiccode`

Wrap them in `className="latin" dir="ltr"` inside Arabic prose — see `styles/rtl.css`.
Without the isolation, bidi drags trailing punctuation to the wrong end of the line.

## Conventions

- **Numerals: Western 0–9** (client decision). Not Arabic-Indic ٠–٩. This matches
  procurement practice in the UAE and keeps the phone number consistent.
- **Plurals** go through i18next's Arabic rules — all six categories
  (`zero one two few many other`). The `one`/`two` forms carry the number
  lexically ("بند واحد"), so they omit `{{count}}` on purpose; `scripts/i18n-check.mjs`
  knows this and does not flag it.
- **Never letter-space Arabic** — it breaks the cursive joins. `styles/rtl.css`
  forces `letter-spacing: 0` on every label that is spaced in English.
- **Never uppercase Arabic** — it is a no-op that only signals a careless port.
- Keep CTAs to the same length class as English. "Get a quote" → «اطلب عرض سعر»,
  not a seven-word phrase.
